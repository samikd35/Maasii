# UI and UX references for the Ethiopian table menu

Public pages and demos checked on 9 November 2026. These references cover distinct parts of the guest experience. The proposed product combines bilingual browsing, orders sent to the assigned waiter, external payment details and private feedback.

## Design directions to choose from

**A. The Printed Menu.** The guest page feels like a contemporary café menu. Warm ivory, dark ink, one muted coffee or olive accent, a distinctive heading and a readable typeface for Amharic and English. On mobile the first dishes appear under a compact venue header and category strip, with prices aligned and a single featured photo. As guests add items, the order summary becomes the main action. They confirm the quantities and see who will receive the order. Sam sees a clean list led by the next table that needs attention. The owner sees a calm floor summary and a separate Trends view. This direction depends on consistent typesetting and a small set of venue-approved photos. [Duka](https://duka.et/r/demo), [Dishoom](https://www.dishoom.com/menu/breakfast/), [ResoFlow demo](https://resoflow.co.uk/demo/order)

**B. The Dish Gallery.** The featured dish is the first thing a guest sees. Use restrained chrome, warm neutrals and consistent crops of actual servings. Dish details show the portion, sides and a short optional video where movement explains something useful. Keep the full list one tap away. Sam’s phone stays compact, while the manager can open a dish to compare what guests viewed, ordered and reviewed. This direction suits a venue that can regularly photograph its food. Photography consistency and load time will matter more here. A still and text carry the same information with motion disabled. [Din Tai Fung dish detail](https://dtf.com/en-us/menu/chicken-xiao-long-bao), [Menu.video demo](https://demo.menu.video/)

**C. The Service Board.** The guest menu is a compact catalogue with clear category labels and a visible order summary. For Sam, the first view groups tables by action, with new orders at the top and payment due clearly labelled. A manager can use a room map on a wider screen, while a phone shows the same statuses in a sorted list. Use high-contrast neutrals and a single brand accent, with status words beside any status colour. This direction suits busier venues where opening the right table quickly matters most. The floor map has a setup cost, so the mobile list is the pattern to validate first. [Toast Tables](https://pos.toasttab.com/products/toast-tables), [Square floor plans](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/6427-building-your-floor-plan), [Linear Inbox](https://linear.app/docs/inbox)

I recommend **A for the guest experience and the waiter queue from C**. Put the venue’s personality in the menu and its photography. Let table numbers, timestamps and clearly named actions carry the waiter interface. Give the owner a live floor view and a separate Trends view with comparable periods and links to the figures. Use the same language for table, order and bill states across all three.

Compare each direction using the same tasks before choosing. Can a guest find a fasting dish, understand its portion and send it to Sam? Can Sam spot the oldest new order and tell which served table has a bill due? Can the manager find the evidence for a service or dish observation? Test the layouts with real Amharic and English content, a narrow phone, and touch targets with enough room. Use explicit labels with status colour and show a text acknowledgement when an order state changes. [W3C target size guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum), [W3C use of colour guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html)

### A focused design choice

Choose the guest's starting layout separately from the staff and owner tools. Compare **Menu first**, a typographic price-led list for fast scanning, with **Dish first**, a gallery of three venue specialties above the full menu. Use the same compact **My tables** queue and **Live floor / Trends** split in both. Compare the variants with a guest finding a fasting dish, sending it to Sam, finding the right payment details, and leaving a dish rating. Then check whether Sam can find and confirm the bill, and whether the owner can follow a finding back to orders and reviews.
