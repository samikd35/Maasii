# Menu software design for Ethiopian hospitality

**Recommended concept.** Give every dish a guest-facing presentation and an owner-facing performance record. The guest can understand the portion and what comes with it, then leave quick feedback about that exact dish. The owner can see which dishes people explored, what the team recorded as ordered, and what guests reported by service period. The proposed value is deciding what to feature, prepare, or improve next week using evidence from the same menu. The first customer to test is an independent, waiter-led café or restaurant that already has a repeatable breakfast and lunch service. Hardware logistics are separate from this design.

**Guest experience and publishing.** The mobile page opens to a fast bilingual menu with service-period sections, clear prices in birr, availability and visible Feedback and Payment options destinations. The featured dish card answers three questions: what will arrive, how much it is, and what is included. Use an actual venue-approved serving photo, a portion cue, short ingredient text and sides or extras. Amharic and English are pilot defaults, with venue-approved descriptions and labels. “I tried this” preselects a named dish and asks “Would you order it again?” Guests can instead rate the whole visit. Either review can include an optional issue chip and comment. Keep feedback private to the venue and available without a guest account.

The owner can draft and publish menu edits in one screen. Preview uses the same guest layout and lets the owner check the language, service period, prices, availability, media and payment directory before publishing. Preview traffic is excluded from guest analytics. Start with owner-set categories and featured dishes. Automatic scheduling is a useful follow-up where operators actually run different service menus. Retain a stable dish identifier and record the published version for changes to price, serving or media.

Payment options list only the outlet’s approved methods and recipient details. Where supported, give the guest a copyable account number or merchant code and display a provider-issued QR as appropriate. The amount comes from the bill. Payment and confirmation happen in the venue’s existing process. Log changes to payment details and label navigation events as directory opens or copy actions. They cannot establish whether a payment was attempted or completed. [telebirr merchant instructions](https://www.ethiotelecom.et/telebirr/%E1%88%88%E1%8B%95%E1%89%83%E1%8B%8E%E1%89%BD-%E1%8A%A5%E1%8A%93-%E1%8A%A0%E1%8C%88%E1%88%8D%E1%8C%8E%E1%89%B6%E1%89%BD-%E1%8B%AD%E1%8A%AD%E1%8D%88%E1%88%89/), [NBE QR standard](https://nbe.gov.et/eipqrc/)

**A table visit connects the guest, waiter and manager.** The table number identifies the physical table. Staff open a visit for the current party and close it after service. Several guests can send orders from their phones into the same visit. Each order has its own items and status. A closed visit remains in staff history, and a new party starts with a fresh bill. The proposed pilot uses one bill per visit.

1. At Table 08 a guest selects dishes and confirms the order. The page offers **Send to Sam** when Sam is assigned to the table and on shift. The guest can use the floor team if the assignment is unclear. The server saves the order before alerting the waiter. The guest sees **Sent** first and **Accepted by Sam** after acknowledgement.
2. Sam signs in on a phone and sees a queue grouped by table. New orders appear first, with quantities, notes and time waiting. Accepted and served orders remain visible on the table card. A floor lead can see unacknowledged orders and hand a table to another waiter. Make claiming and transferring a visit atomic. A retried guest submission uses the same request identifier so a weak connection does not duplicate dishes.
3. Preparing the bill locks its amount for collection. An additional order requires reopening the bill before service continues. An authorised waiter or cashier confirms payment after checking the cash or recipient-side payment record for the full amount. Save the amount, method, staff member and time against the current bill revision, and make repeated confirmation safe. Keep bill status separate from order status. Bind submissions to the visit they started in, and reject orders on a closed visit. Staff access stays limited to their outlet and role. A guest page shows that guest’s order status and the approved payment details.
4. The guest can review the visit, a dish, or the service they received. Link service feedback to the visit and the waiter who handled the relevant interaction. Keep the assignment history so a later handoff preserves earlier feedback and acknowledgement times. Menu-only reviews can remain open. Waiter-specific feedback uses the service record to identify the waiter.

The manager can add or archive dishes, publish menu edits, assign and hand off tables, and see unacknowledged orders and unpaid bills. Archiving a dish preserves its history. Record party size at seating if the venue needs a headcount. Start waiter reporting with assigned tables by shift, time to acknowledge, outstanding bills and service feedback with sample sizes. Compare similar service periods and keep dish or kitchen issues separate from waiter service feedback. Bill confirmation records who confirmed payment. The assignment history records who served the table.

**Three data sources answer different questions.**

| Signal | Collection | What owners can learn |
|---|---|---|
| Browsing | Public menu opens, explicit dish detail opens and optional media plays, timestamped. | Attention by item and time. Repeat views are possible. |
| Recorded orders | The waiter queue records accepted dish quantities and timestamps. Staff can also enter orders taken in person. | Accepted units by service period on covered tables. Exclude declined or cancelled items. Served units and paid bills use their own status and timestamps. |
| Feedback | Private visit or dish response, rating, tags and optional comment. | What respondents reported. Group by submission period unless the guest states when they ate or the feedback has a reliable link to an order. |

Set service windows in the outlet’s local time, with comparable service windows when reporting changes. Show the coverage of the order source beside every order view. An explicit zero is different from a missing shift. Match imports to the correct dish and period, review unknown items and repeated batches, and use one source of record per period to avoid double counting. Use recorded order quantities or served quantities according to the source and label them accordingly. Compare browsing and orders across covered service periods, and avoid describing separate counts as a conversion rate. When availability is known, show it in the item drill-down. A sold-out item can attract views after the kitchen runs out, so logged orders alone cannot estimate unmet demand.

**Dashboard that leads to a decision.** Start with the service-period filter, a chart of dish detail opens, a table of recorded units and feedback counts, and one written finding. Add a weekday-by-service heatmap when the dataset is large enough to reveal a useful staffing or prep pattern. Selecting a dish can show its recurring issues, published changes and supporting comments. For example, if five of 16 self-selected responses about mushroom tibs mention temperature, show the counts and suggest checking plating-to-service time. Track the change the manager made and compare comparable weeks afterward. The evidence can flag a pattern worth investigating. It cannot diagnose its cause.

Calculate counts, percentages, coverage and time comparisons in application code. Use AI to classify or translate redacted Amharic, English and mixed-language comments, and draft a weekly note backed by the computed figures and review references. Show the original alongside a translation and let the manager correct the classification. Sample small batches with a fluent reviewer first. When orders or review volume are sparse, show the available evidence and flag missing coverage or small samples. Forecasting requires stable, sufficiently complete order and availability history. Before choosing external processing for personal data, apply the locally relevant hosting and transfer requirements. [Ethiopian data protection proclamation, Article 22](https://justice.gov.et/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/seasiisia-seusiNse%C2%A1-1321-2016.pdf)

**Media ladder.** Start with an honest photo of the serving guests receive and concise text for each dish. Test an optional silent 5–8 second plate, pour or cut-open clip on a few items where the photo cannot convey portion, texture or presentation. Keep the text and poster available, use native video controls, fetch the clip on request and show a still fallback. For an outlet where scale matters, try one interactive 3D model after the video test. Capture a venue-approved serving, load a compressed GLB behind a poster on interaction with a viewer such as model-viewer, and keep an image fallback. A separately calibrated AR experiment could test portion scale. Media production, refresh work and mobile performance are costs. Compare guest understanding and staff questions between photo and richer previews before expanding. [Web video loading](https://web.dev/articles/lazy-loading-video), [model-viewer loading examples](https://modelviewer.dev/examples/loading/index.html)

International benchmarks confirm that the individual ideas already exist. Toast documents item-level product mix from sales records, including an hour filter. Ovation reports guest experience by items present in POS orders, which differs from directly asking someone to rate a named dish. TableQR advertises dish-linked feedback in its menu. The angle to validate is the combination of draft preview, direct feedback and clearly attributed daypart data in a standalone menu for waiter-led venues. The public sources cannot establish exclusivity or willingness to pay. [Toast menu reports](https://support.toasttab.com/en/article/Menu-Report-Overview-1492794696577), [Ovation item performance report](https://intercom.help/ovation-bd4a0805515d/en/articles/15503353-menu-item-performance-report), [TableQR guest feedback](https://tableqr.co/features/customer-feedbacks/)

**Build sequence.** Pilot one outlet with a guest menu, owner preview and publishing, waiter assignment, the table queue and payment confirmation. Give staff a way to enter orders taken in person. Start with the live floor view and private visit, dish and service feedback. Use accepted item counts to compare breakfast and lunch once enough tables are covered. Measure missed orders, time to acknowledge, unpaid bills at close and whether staff can use the queue during a busy service. Add the written analytics note after the order and feedback data are dependable.

## Ethiopian software benchmarks

Public first-party sources checked on 9 November 2026, Addis Ababa time. Product pages establish what vendors advertise. Public demos show parts of the guest or editing experience. Logged-in dashboards, installations and usage figures were outside this review.

| Product | What the public sources establish | What remains open |
|---|---|---|
| **QR Menu Et** | Its annual plans advertise a visitor dashboard separately from advanced visitor and sales analytics. The privacy policy describes recording a timestamp and referring QR code ID. Owners can change prices, add specials and toggle sold-out status. [Product and plans](https://qrmenu.et/), [Privacy policy](https://qrmenu.et/privacy) | Which hourly or daypart views owners see, how repeat openings are counted and how sales are attributed. |
| **Addis QR Menu** | The indexed first-party page advertises scan trends, popular-item insights, guest ratings and quick feedback. [Addis QR Menu](https://addis-menu.com/) | What makes a dish popular, who can see feedback, and whether ratings refer to a dish or the visit. |
| **Easymaz** | The order, kitchen and table system advertises top items, busiest hours, average order and Excel export. It records waiter and table QR orders, plus their payment status. Its current page describes a separate fiscal register and a comparison at closing. [Easymaz](https://www.easymaz.app/), [QR ordering workflow](https://www.easymaz.app/qr-menu/) | The top-item formula, such as units versus revenue, and exactly which order or payment states count. |
| **Tapserve** | Its Pro POS plan advertises table QR ordering, customer feedback and detailed reports. Its published flow sends the QR order through cashier approval to the kitchen. [Tapserve](https://www.tapserve.et/) | Whether feedback can be private, anonymous and dish-specific, and which reporting filters exist. |
| **Mesob Menu** | This Addis-based product advertises NFC and QR access, guest reviews and basic analytics. The same page labels the product alpha access and early-adopter beta. [Mesob Menu](https://mesobmenu.com/) | Deployment maturity, how reviews reach staff, and the analytics available to owners. |
| **Get Menu** | Its public page demonstrates price and sold-out changes on a guest-facing dish card. It advertises paper-menu photo import with review before publishing, branding controls and mobile edits. [Get Menu](https://getmenu.et/) | Whether signed-in owners can preview all draft changes in the live guest layout before publishing. |

ZMenu also advertises a drag-and-drop catalog, automatic breakfast and lunch scheduling, daily shift closeout and dish revenue trends. Scheduled menu content and analytics grouped by time are separate features. In the local sources reviewed, photos are well documented, while support for playable dish videos and interactive 3D dish models was unverified. Outside Ethiopia, GoPlate shows photos, video and manipulable 3D in a guest demo. Menu.video advertises NFC and QR entry plus dish watch-time reporting. These are feature precedents, and Ethiopian demand and performance remain to be tested. [ZMenu](https://zmenu.et/), [GoPlate](https://goplate.app/), [Menu.video](https://menu.video/)

The opportunity to test is a standalone menu that helps a manager distinguish **when guests open the menu**, **which dishes draw attention**, and **what diners report about those dishes** during ordinary waiter-led service. There is already public evidence of local competitors offering the individual features, including table access, reports and feedback. The review did not verify a local dashboard combining all three signals with clear metric definitions and a guest preview of unpublished changes. Sales rankings require an order or POS source with known coverage. For the prior research on privacy and dish-level feedback workflows, see [Ethiopian table menu and feedback research](table-menu-feedback-research.md).
